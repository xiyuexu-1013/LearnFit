# LearnFit: student testing and contribution record

This is a blank protocol, not evidence of completed user research. The updates made with the assistant must be disclosed as AI-assisted; they do not establish the student's personal coding contribution.

## Small usability pilot

Invite 5–10 willing classmates to try one real on-screen reading task for 10–15 minutes. Explain that eye behavior is not a measure of attention or learning. They can decline camera access or stop at any time. Keep notes under participant codes, without names, video, or eye measurements. Do not collect account credentials or health information.

Ask each participant to:
1. Explain what they think the app does after reading the home screen.
2. Enter a reading task and start calibration.
3. Use LearnFit beside their reading in a separate visible window.
4. Pause, resume, and briefly switch tabs to inspect automatic pausing.
5. End the session and explain the report in their own words.
6. Optionally save a reflection on their own browser and locate the delete option.

For each session record:
- Participant code, date, browser/device, glasses yes/no only if voluntarily relevant
- Calibration completed: yes/no; approximate time; obstacle if any
- Pause/resume understood: yes/no; confusing wording
- Report understood as eye-behavior feedback rather than an attention score: yes/no
- One useful aspect; one frustrating aspect; willingness to use again and why
- Your planned change, actual implementation, and retest result

Report counts honestly, e.g. “3 of 5 participants could not find Resume before the label change.” A small usability pilot cannot establish improvements in academic performance or clinical validity.

## Personal development log

For each change you personally complete, record:
- Date and the concrete problem
- Your own design decision and why
- Files/functions you wrote or changed
- What assistance or external code you used (including AI)
- The test you ran, result, and remaining limitations
- A screenshot or dated source snapshot

Suggested personally owned next task: investigate individual blink thresholds using labeled, consented test observations. Keep the existing detector as a comparison, explain the threshold choice, measure missed/extra detections, and document conditions where it fails. Do not claim this feature exists until it is implemented and tested.

## Known limits after the workflow update

- Blink detection still uses the inherited EAR threshold of 0.22. Individual threshold adaptation needs labeled validation before replacing it.
- Camera tracking has not been validated across representative devices, eye shapes, glasses, and lighting conditions.
- Automated tests verify software behavior, not attention accuracy or learning outcomes.
- Local saved summaries do not sync between devices and can disappear if browser storage is cleared.
- No real participant results or student-authored implementation claims have been generated by the assistant.
